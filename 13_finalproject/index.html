While the final iteration marked real progress from my prototype demonstrated during MVP week, it also revealed several complications, many of which were the result of transitioning from a quick prototyping mindset to a more “finished product” approach.One major change was my decision to move from a breadboard to a protoboard, soldering all my wiring connections for permanent connections. In theory, this was the right move toward a more stable circuit. In practice, my inexperience with soldering proved costly. I applied too much solder in certain places, and this likely caused unintended bridges between connections, leading to shorting the entire circuit. I had checked with a TF to confirm whether or not the circuit had shorted with a multimeter and was able to confirm that more than likely it was shorted. The ESP32 also was very hot to touch after I had finished soldering all the wire connections. Physical constraints also became an issue. I had designed the platform for the plant pot early on, before finalizing the wiring and soldered components. Once the soldering was complete, I realized the now bulkier circuit no longer fit under the box platform as intended. <p> <p>Another intended upgrade was switching from the small OLED screen used in early versions to a larger TFT display for better readability and a more polished look. Unfortunately, despite numerous attempts ranging from tweaking the code to rewriting display functions entirely, I was unable to get the TFT screen to display the plant’s sensor data information. Even after multiple code iterations and troubleshooting sessions, the display remained blank; a white screen, forcing me to stick with the smaller OLED in the final version.<p> <p>Finally, wire management proved to be an ongoing challenge. As the number of components increased, so did the wiring complexity. Without a clear, consistent labeling system, it became time-consuming to trace wires back to their corresponding sensors or modules. This not only slowed down debugging but also made the assembly look less organized than I had hoped. These pitfalls underscored a key lesson for me which was: moving from a functional prototype to a polished build requires not only technical skill but also careful planning. There are some things I had not accounted for in the process that looking back, I wish I had paid attention to.Although there were many complications I plan to continue making adjustments, fixing multiple components, and reworking certain peices to this project to refine things and hopefully make it more ploished and ready to oficially use.<p> NEXT STEPS: <br> <p>Some next steps in refining the “smart plant pot” would be to create a slot in the pot CAD design, using Fusion 360, where the OLED screen could be embedded directly into the pot’s body.
