I decided to remvamp my final project idea. My original plan prior to this assignment was to create a seed planting robotic arm that plants seeds and also tests for things like soil health, humidity, light intensity, and temperature. This idea unfortunately did not end up working and I have decided not to follow through with it (this will also be updated in my offical final project page of the website). The reason for not following through with the project was due to time and structure of the class, and how many components would go into a project of that kind. I had started immediately last Thursday after class on the CAD for the robotic arm but as days went on I started to realize how many components went into the project and how many errors were already occuring, along with many parts that I did not have access to or would need to buy. This made me worried about the ability to complete an MVP in time for the deadline as well as the ability to actually continue on with the project smoothly and have something for the project fair. With working through it more and also talking with Bobby(one of the TF's for this class) to gain more insight, supoort, and feedback on next steps, we collectively decided to scratch the robotic arm idea and do something more feasible within the given time frame. As mentioned early the main functions thus far for this smart pot is to indicate and track soil health, as well as provide humidity and temperature values and indicate when there is too much humidity for example or the temperature is too high. <br> MAIN components: <br> -ESP32 microcontroller <br> -Breadboard <br> -Wires, resistors <br> -4 LED lights(Red, green, blue, and yellow) <br> -Capacitive Soil moisture sensor <br> -OLED display screen <br> <br> I started off with creating code to do some calibration with the sensor and get values for air and water to see if the sensor was working properly. I tested this by holding the sensor downright in air and then tracking values on the serial monitor and averaging the values to produce one "airvalue". After that I did the same but this time submerging the sensor in a cup of water and then tracking values on the serial monitor and averaging those values to produce one "watervalue". <br> The values I got where: <br> airvalue: 3664 <br> watervalue: 1076 <br> These values fluctuated quite a bit doing three trials and I am not completely sure if these values are ideal or if noise and environmental factors may be getting in the way of calibration. <br> <br> After completing calibration for air and water values I started adding on to the code. My next add on was getting the OLED display screen to work. I made sure to wire it properly to the board. Wiring the sensor and screen to the breadboard made me realize that there was going to be a lot more components all of which required power and ground and the ESP32 only has so many spots for power and ground. I decided to connect the ESP32 pins of power and ground to the rails on the breadboard along with other power and ground conenctions for the sensor and the screen that way the rails would act as power and ground all the way through giving me more space to work with other components such as the LED's. The LED's were wired with resistors to create current drops so that the current coming in was supported by the ESP32. <br> <br> In my code I designed it so that there are thresholds for the amount of water in the soil. This gives the user a more real-time interactive feel to the water process so that they can better understand more efficently understand watering the plant for maximized plant health. This is then all displayed on the screen. The thresholds for soil information are as follows: <br> More water needed: 30% or < <br> Moist, water to soil ratio is sufficent: 31%-79% <br> Too much water: 80% or > <br> Each LED light is directly connected to these thresholds. So when the red LED light turns on this means that more water is needed. When the green LED light turns on this indicates water values are mostly sufficent and when the blue LED light turns on this indicates too much water has been added. <br> Here are pictures of the code in its entirety: <br> <img style="border: 3px solid #eb85e7; box-shadow: #d53aa8 5px 5px 7px 4px;" src="code-1.jpeg" alt="generic placeholder image"> <br> <br> <img style="border: 3px solid #eb85e7; box-shadow: #d53aa8 5px 5px 7px 4px;" src="code-2.jpeg" alt="generic placeholder image"> <br> <br> <img style="border: 3px solid #eb85e7; box-shadow: #d53aa8 5px 5px 7px 4px;" src="code-3.jpeg" alt="generic placeholder image"> <br> <br> <img style="border: 3px solid #eb85e7; box-shadow: #d53aa8 5px 5px 7px 4px;" src="code-4.jpeg" alt="generic placeholder image"> <br> <br> Here is a pictures of the wiring: <br> <img style="border: 3px solid #eb85e7; box-shadow: #d53aa8 5px 5px 7px 4px;" src="wire-1.jpeg" alt="generic placeholder image"> <br> <br> <img style="border: 3px solid #eb85e7; box-shadow: #d53aa8 5px 5px 7px 4px;" src="wire-2.jpeg" alt="generic placeholder image"> <br> <br> Another thing I added was a progress bar.
